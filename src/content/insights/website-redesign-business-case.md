---
title: How to decide whether a website redesign is worth it
description: A better business case for a redesign starts with evidence about customers, content and performance.
published: 2026-08-14
category: Website development
---

A redesign is easy to justify with a sentence like “the site feels dated”. It is much harder to make a good investment decision from that sentence alone.

The useful question is not whether the website could look better. It is where the current website is getting in the way of a business result.

## Find the friction first

Speak to the people who use the site and the people who deal with the consequences. Ask:

- What do prospective customers regularly misunderstand?
- Which questions arrive by phone or email because the site did not answer them?
- Which services or products are difficult to find?
- Where do enquiries go wrong or arrive without enough information?
- Which pages are staff reluctant to send to customers?

These answers produce better redesign priorities than a list of visual preferences. They also reveal when the real problem is pricing, positioning, internal follow-up or missing content rather than layout.

## Set a small number of outcomes

Choose two or three outcomes that can guide decisions. Examples include more suitable enquiries, fewer basic support questions, clearer recruitment information or a higher proportion of visitors reaching a booking page.

Avoid treating page views as the whole story. A busy website is not necessarily a useful one. The right measure depends on the job of the page and the next step you want a person to take.

## Audit the content before designing screens

Most redesigns have more content than they need and less useful information than they should. Review each important page and decide whether it should be:

- kept and improved;
- combined with another page;
- rewritten around a real customer question;
- moved somewhere else; or
- removed.

This prevents the new design becoming a polished container for old confusion. It also makes the project easier to estimate because the work is visible before development begins.

## Protect what already works

A redesign should not assume that everything existing is wrong. Check search visibility, useful landing pages, enquiry routes, accessibility, loading performance and links that other people rely on. Record important URLs and redirects before anything moves.

The best result is not the most dramatic visual change. It is a site that is easier to understand, easier to maintain and more effective at helping the right people act.

If the evidence does not show a meaningful problem, improve the weakest pages first. A focused content and conversion project may be a better investment than replacing the whole site.
